\subsection{Explicit control and conditional preparation}
\label{sec:v4-progress}
The guarantees below do not require every region to receive service.
They also do not assign request persistence to the consensus layer.

\begin{proposition}[No unannounced cross-region Force]
\label{prop:explicit-force}
Starting from an authenticated checkpoint, every subsequent increment
of $\beta$ has a unique effective MSIG cause in the verified L1 control
history. A status update, missing DATA, local health report or Ready event
does not increment $\beta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Protocol~5 step~3 is the only transition that increments $\beta$.
It is reached only for the first valid due instruction matching the
captured entry version. Control identities and persisted subpositions
prevent a second application. Other transitions either change
eligibility, resolve a boundary, append DATA or update local programs.
None changes $\beta$. Induction over committed transitions proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Preparation under a continuing assignment]
\label{prop:ready-bound}
Let a public assignment open in block $J_o$, with finalization time
$t_0$. If the selected region and its state service satisfy
\cref{ass:ready-service}, the fixed handoff remains applicable, and no
Force supersedes it during preparation, correct target members can
complete Ready/Enter by $t_0+T_{\rm ready}$.
Entry uses the retained anchor of that assignment and occurs at most
once per member and pair. No DATA production is required for entry.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An earlier initial Enter already satisfies the claim. For a member
that has not entered, the service assumption supplies every material
condition of \cref{def:ready} by the stated time. Continuing applicability supplies
the separate current-entry predicate. Protocols~3 and~4 then perform the
guarded entry in the same complete event.
\Cref{prop:program-isolation} supplies anchor binding and at-most-once
entry. The gate does not inspect future DATA output. A valid Force
before completion would remove the continuing-assignment premise,
not violate a mandatory waiting rule.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What is needed for global progress}
\label{sec:v4-global-progress}
The regional engine keeps the Part~I conditions for actual correct-leader
turns, eventual equivocation exclusion and uninterrupted commit
opportunities. Under those conditions, a session that continues running
can form a complete result. The bounded Ready service alone does not
establish that uninterrupted regional opportunity.

If a complete result is available at time $s$, the declared services give
submission by $s+T_{\rm pub}$ and Espresso visibility by
$s+T_{\rm pub}+T_E$. This is transport of that result. To advance $G$,
the result must supply the next required DATA under its actual fixed
boundary, while its producing version remains open at the canonical
acceptance position. References and predecessor dependencies must pass
the checks of \cref{sec:exports}.
The public-kernel and per-DATA rules ensure that false outer hints or
a later unresolved successor do not erase an otherwise valid next
DATA transaction. They do not permit a gap or a closed-version append.

With these regional, transport, acceptance and control-service conditions,
replay of the suitable result advances $G$. The service bounds do not
give a wall-clock processing deadline for every arbitrary observer.
No unconditional global-growth theorem follows merely from repeated
Ready events, published raw records or committee honesty.

\paragraph{Offline recovery requires control availability.}
When the current region cannot continue, progress needs the committee
to submit a suitable MSIG, disable a failed candidate when necessary,
and leave or enable a live eligible successor. If no such control is
submitted, there is no automatic fallback. If all regions are OFF,
the boundary can remain unassigned indefinitely. If MSIGs repeatedly
close each useful interval, no regional output need become global.
These are allowed executions unless the governance service supplies
the additional liveness conditions.

\paragraph{Failure and safety are separate.}
An offline region does not satisfy the operational premises of the
preparation and regional-service claims. A violated lifetime Byzantine
weight bound is a different failure of the certificate model.
Neither case permits multisig to roll back $G$.
No theorem here promises fair service among regions, automatic ON,
one DATA between Forces or eventual inclusion of a client request.
