# Complexity, latency, retention and receiver audit — rewrite v1

Audited frozen source: `formalization/build/rewrite/review-v1.tex` (1,244 lines). Read the model, objects, algorithms, claims, Section 7 and authenticated-broadcast appendix. No manuscript edits. The findings below concern the written protocol; suggested changes are not assumed to exist already.

## Findings requiring correction or clarification

1. **[P2] Distinct-body traffic bound needs finer caching and duplicate-transfer rules.** Lines **391–394, 518–524** identify/cache complete objects, whereas **1117–1124** bound all successful body transfer by the sum of missing *distinct bytes*, then by `(a+1)M`. Several origin reports can contain the same mini-block range under different report identities; a receiver following the stated object-level rule can download those bytes again. Concurrent primary push and pull can also deliver two successful copies. The stated bound is valid for a deduplicated body/chunk transport, but that rule is not yet explicit. Specify a body identity shared across proposal/report/chain wrappers, requests only for missing constituent bodies, and handling of already-outstanding duplicate transfers; alternatively count repeated successful transfers separately. This also affects the claimed savings at **1130–1136**.

2. **[P2] Announcement period is used as an end-to-end discovery bound.** `T_A` is a transmission period at **221, 509–511**, but **983–984** and **1037** claim discovery within `T_A`, including propagation. A tip becoming relevant just after the last periodic announcement can need almost one whole period plus network delay; even an immediate new-certificate announcement can take more than `T_A` absent a parameter relation. Define an end-to-end discovery budget separately, or add the announcement path delay. Apply the same choice consistently to `L_sync`, finalized delivery, repair and geographic recovery (**996, 1024, 1051, 1066**).

3. **[P2] The local failed-leader formula needs a narrower failure class.** The bound `(q K_R+1) tau_R + 4 delta_R + delta_P` at **1045–1051** is justified for `q` continuously unproductive/crashed leaders whose completed instances keep returning bottom. It is not a bound to the first *successor* preview for arbitrary Byzantine leaders: a non-bottom outcome resets the counter (**574–579**), so a leader can alternate some valid service with gaps and remain until the planned boundary `N_R`, which may greatly exceed `K_R`. State the continuous-failure condition; for arbitrary Byzantine tenure, account for the planned tenure. This does not negate the first-reportable-batch intuition behind `L_start`: intermittent non-bottom service itself supplies a batch, even before reaching an honest leader.

4. **[P2] Receiver handoff needs an explicit anti-replay/monotonic-authority guard.** The consumer state at **402, 504–507** has no specified newest verified global anchor, and D6 at **535–537** unconditionally says to adopt a finalized rotation's generation. An old genuine ROTATE proof remains cryptographically valid; replaying it must not move the current authority backward or discard a later generation's preview. D5's delivered-prefix guard protects the final log but does not explicitly guard this authority update. Require that a handoff extend the consumer's current verified control/activation anchor, and ignore already-consumed or ancestor activations. The soundness claim at **889–911** should refer to that explicit receiver rule. This is a missing receiver guard, not a counterexample to global certificate uniqueness.

5. **[P2, accounting completeness] The table excludes recurring control traffic without a total expression.** The four component rows at **1099–1102** correctly count their named primitives, but optional HEAD traffic (**405–410**), periodic/on-change announcements (**511**), repair requests (**518–524**) and evidence propagation (**748**) are only described as additional traffic (**1126**). No slot duration or per-interval count is supplied for HEAD. Either call the table a subtotal and parameterize these extra events explicitly, or define their cadences/quotas and add them to the interval cost. An event-count expression avoids inventing a rate assumption.

6. **[P3, capacity interpretation] Nominal report maxima are not a guaranteed service rate.** The report is an indivisible earliest-prefix packing of outcomes (**670–671**). Therefore both offered rates being below `b_G/T_G` and `B_G/T_G` (**988–993**) is necessary but not generally sufficient for a bounded backlog. For example, with count cap 10, byte cap 10 units and every outcome 6 units, only one outcome fits per epoch; arrival of 1.5 outcomes/epoch is below both nominal maxima but exceeds actual service. The equations are conditional on the separately assumed finite `Q`, so this is not by itself a counterexample to them. Make clear that `Q` is a workload/service assumption, or give a guaranteed packing/service bound incorporating maximum outcome size and bursts.

## Checks that passed, with their scope

- **Metadata versus complete data:** **160–176, 432–437, 597–599, 1182–1185** explicitly require full-body availability before processing. Hence the `2 delta_R` result is a *complete-object* good case; **1018–1019** correctly exclude a bare announcement. The round bounds assume enough concurrent transfer, verification and supplier capacity for all prescribed parallel broadcasts. They are not derivable from hop diameter alone.
- **Serialized local completion:** `3 Delta_R + n_i Delta_R + 2 Delta_R` matches the stated `(n_i+5) Delta_R` spacing (**441–452, 467–470, 484–491**). The extra complete-instance allowance in the continuous-failure recovery expression is conservative.
- **READY and fixed start:** READY binds the parent's precise tip (**371–375**), ROTATE retains that tip (**650–661**), and CONFIRM cannot advance it (**642–644**). With `T_G >= (N+3) Delta_G`, processing finality by `(N+2) Delta_G` leaves at least one global-delay margin before the fixed activation boundary (**621–627, 693–703**). This is sound for a healthy, synchronized participating successor. READY alone does not guarantee continued availability; **707–712** correctly say so. A cold node missing the proof/data cannot start; any cold-transfer timing claim remains conditional on obtaining the current parent and full required history, not merely its old READY signature.
- **Geographic recovery accounting:** **1057–1078** explicitly charges late-tail adoption, readiness, ROTATE/CONFIRM and subsequent local service. Its endpoint before adding equation (delivery) is a successor **Preview**; “first successor delivery” at **1070** should use that name to avoid conflating the two interfaces. The formula is conditional on the supplied tail, transfer and successor bounds, not a universal outage bound.
- **DS and backbone costs:** With two lifetime forwards per `(origin, member)` (**1204**), there are `O(n^2)` multicasts. The direct `O(n^3)` and flooded `O(E n^2)` transmission bounds and their signature-chain bit factors (**1081–1102**) are consistent. They properly exclude arbitrary Byzantine wire flooding and parameterize growing evidence (**1109–1114**).
- **Retention and repair:** Global holders retain adopted data before voting and throughout the configuration (**196–205, 617, 679–680**). The per-object repair lemma correctly needs a reachable honest holder and a bounded number of preceding failed attempts (**914–928**). Whole-history sync is not one bounded request; **983–986, 1138–1143** correctly sum chunks and retain the history-size dependence. Global liveness still needs always-responsive honest weight greater than two thirds (**178–192**).
