CHANGES
1. Actual message delay is delta<=Delta and may vary across messages.
   The model uses a common global clock; node-specific entry events can differ.
2. Basic signature/hash and honest-state-retention assumptions were not added
   as explicit premises, as requested. The complete-message processing bound
   and common initialization/initial entry-skew condition remain explicit.
3. L1 is an ideal public bulletin board. At finite common time d(g), AUTH_g is
   finalized and simultaneously available to all correct replicas, including
   frozen replicas. L1 progress remains independent of L2 commitment.
4. g_bad is the generation named by the first admissible equivocation proof
   revealed for identity p, not the current generation at revelation.
   Removal is permanent from finite G_p>g_bad; no fixed gap bound is asserted.
5. Protocol 2 reads AUTH from L1 and uses max(a_r+Delta,d(g+1)).
   Frozen-state processing and authority validation reflect the bulletin board.
6. Entry skew is proved from the shared d(g+1), replacing the old relay proof.
7. Finite equivocation-generation reasoning uses the finite ranges g<G_p and
   distinguishes late proof revelation from the generation named by the proof.
8. Recovery latency uses earliest entry max(a+Delta,d(g+1)), and all-replica
   first-block commitment by that time +8Delta under the surviving-window
   premise. The bound reduces to a+9Delta when d(g+1)<=a+Delta.
9. L1 proof-relay costs were removed from the current protocol accounting;
   bulletin-board access and eligibility updates remain external service costs.
10. The affected counterexample and v3 comparison table were aligned. The
    source date and layouts of the assumption list/recovery paragraph updated.

TARGETED CONSISTENCY CHECK
- With common d, x->max(x+Delta,d) is 1-Lipschitz, so successor entry skew
  remains <=Delta from the proved quit skew.
- A correct timer's protecting QC is still known before each later lock
  snapshot because entry remains >=local quit+Delta.
- An exposed identity cannot have an admissible leader-equivocation position
  at g>=G_p. The finite identity set therefore yields finitely many implicated
  generation indices; late disclosure does not freeze unrelated generations.
- The 2Delta NEWVIEW wait, (2p+4)Delta progress deadlines, and q+Delta handoff
  bounds retain their earlier dependencies. No timer or voting phase was added.
- Existing attack schedules permit different message delays <=Delta and do
  not require clock offsets. The homogeneous-delta performance illustration
  remains expressly a special case.
- No stale d_r/d_s, TODO, or current-protocol authority-relay proof remains.
  The comparison table intentionally describes relay as the historical v3 rule.
