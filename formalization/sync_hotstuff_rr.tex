\textbf{Notation and scope.}
This is the paper-notation presentation of the count-based V4 construction.
As in Sync HotStuff~\cite{sync}, $B_k$ denotes a block at height $k$,
$\cert_v(B_k)$ its certificate of $f+1$ matching signed votes in view $v$,
and a correct replica signs at most one block at each $(v,k)$.
The committee has $n=2f+1$ replicas, at most $f$ Byzantine; correct-to-correct
complete messages, including ancestry and validation, arrive within $\Delta$.
Clocks have equal rates; signatures are unforgeable and $\mathsf H$ is
collision resistant. Signatures bind the message type, view, height,
block hash and parent. The certificate rank retains the paper's notation
$\rank(\cert_v(B_k))=(v,k)$; \RR{equal-rank distinct blocks are ordered by
a fixed digest tie-break when recovery must choose one.}
Correct replicas enter the initial view within $\Delta$ of one another.

\RR{The only added view structure is that $v=(g,j)$ consists of a recovery
generation $g$ and an ordinary leader term $j$. We continue to call this
whole object the view $v$. Normal $v+1$ means $(g,j+1)$; all original
signed-message and timer subscripts still use $v$. Its leader is uniquely
$L(v)=L_{j\bmod n}$, with no independent leader choice. Every generation
starts with a common committed block $A_g$ at height $H_g$ and starting term
$j_g$. Term $j\ge j_g$ owns the $K\ge1$ fresh heights
$H_g+(j-j_g)K+1,\ldots,H_g+(j-j_g+1)K$. Its last certificate is
$N_v=\cert_v(B_{H_g+(j-j_g+1)K})$. A normal entry needs all intervening
terminal certificates.}

\RR{A valid quit proof is either $f+1$ signed $\langle\mathrm{blame},v\rangle$
messages for one admissible current-generation view or two different
leader-signed proposals at the same $(v,k)$ with conflicting block hashes
and complete verifiable ancestry. Two incompatible current-generation
histories expose such a pair at their first divergent canonical slot.
Re-encoding one block or signing it twice does not give this proof.
Admissibility requires a signed ancestry from $A_g$ through the prescribed
term heights; a quit proof for $v=(g,j)$ includes its authenticated entry
history. A STATUS is valid only if it is signed for generation $g$, contains
at least one such valid quit proof, and includes only verifiable
current-generation QCs; malformed inputs yield $\bot$.}
A member keeps at most one pending local BLAME complaint. Sending one marks
it pending; authenticated normal or recovery entry clears that local flag,
without erasing a previously signed BLAME message.

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=.65pt,innertopmargin=5pt,innerbottommargin=5pt]
\textbf{Let $v$ be the current view and $L=L(v)$ its leader. While its
generation is not frozen, a replica $r$ runs the following steps.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*),itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Propose.} If $r=L$, upon obtaining a certificate
$\cert_v(B_{k-1})$, broadcast
$\langle\mathrm{propose},B_k,v,\cert_v(B_{k-1})\rangle_L$, where
$B_k$ extends $B_{k-1}$.
\RR{Use the preceding term's terminal certificate instead for the first
fresh child after normal rotation; use the locally agreed recovery decision
$\decide_{g-1}$ for the first child of a new generation. The leader acts as
soon as the parent is justified and its term still has an unused slot.}

\item \textbf{Vote.} \RR{On any authenticated current-generation proposal
or certificate, including one from an earlier term, compare its complete
signed ancestry with retained histories. If the first divergence exhibits
two proposals signed by the same scheduled leader at one $(v',k')$,
broadcast both original signed proposals with their ancestry and immediately
execute Figure~\ref{fig:rr-change}(i), before another vote.}
Otherwise, upon a valid proposal (possibly relayed), if no leader
equivocation is known, forward it and multicast
$\langle\mathrm{vote},B_k,v\rangle_r$; start
$\mathrm{commit\text{-}timer}_{v,k}=2\Delta$ on the first vote at $(v,k)$.
\RR{Catch up through complete terminal certificates before applying this
step. If already past $B_k$ on the same certified history, relay
$\cert_v(B_k)$ without re-voting. On $N_v$, enter $v+1$ immediately and
let $L(v+1)$ propose; disable the completed term's missing-proposal test.
Keep all old pending commit timers running during this normal rotation.}

\item \textbf{(Non-blocking) Commit.} When
$\mathrm{commit\text{-}timer}_{v,k}$ reaches zero, commit $B_k$ and all its
uncommitted ancestors. \RR{Process quit and equivocation evidence delivered
at that instant first. This callback commits only if no valid quit proof has
frozen the timer's generation; a generation freeze cancels its outstanding
timers. Completed commits are never rolled back.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{mdframed}

timer notation; red text is the RR normal-rotation and cross-term evidence
extension. A vote QC can enable the next proposal before local commit.}
\label{fig:rr-steady}
\end{minipage}

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=.65pt,innertopmargin=5pt,innerbottommargin=5pt]
\textbf{Let $v=(g,j)$ be a view in generation $g$. Each replica $r$
runs the following view-change steps on a complete quit proof.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*),itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Blame and quit view.} If fewer than $p$ valid fresh proposals
are known after $(2p+4)\Delta$ from local entry to $v$, broadcast
$\langle\mathrm{blame},v\rangle_r$ for $1\le p\le K$.
On gathering $f+1$ matching blame signatures, broadcast them.
If leader equivocation is detected, broadcast the two original signed
proposals and their verifying ancestry.
\RR{Either complete witness immediately freezes the \emph{whole current
generation}, even if its blamed term precedes the replica's active term.
Stop new votes and normal entries; cancel every outstanding timer from that
generation. A single BLAME signature does not freeze it. Keep completed commits. While frozen, continue receiving and
forwarding old proposals, votes, QCs and quit proofs, checking them for
additional equivocation and assembling QCs; make no new data vote.}

\item \textbf{Status.} Wait $\Delta$ from the local freeze time $a_r$.
\RR{At $u_r=a_r+\Delta$, sign and send a STATUS containing all known
current-generation quit proofs and certified blocks, with full ancestry.
Run one authenticated broadcast per sender, all in parallel.
Each sender signs and broadcasts its STATUS at its own $u_i$. Each
recipient uses its \emph{own} $u_r$ as the origin for every sender instance:
local round $q$ ends at $u_r+2q\Delta$ and accepts signature chains with
exactly $q$ distinct signers. An honest recipient extends accepted chains
at the start of round $q+1$. Use $f+1$ such rounds, buffering early
chains and rejecting late ones. At the last boundary, output a unique
valid value or $\bot$ for that sender. Call the common vector $\decide_g$.
It contains every correct STATUS.}

\item \textbf{New-view.} \RR{From $\decide_g$, let $b$ be the least blamed
term $j'$ with a valid current-generation quit proof. Independently, pick
a highest-ranked \emph{current-generation} certified block there, using
$A_g$ if none is reported; break equal ranks by the fixed digest rule.
Call its block $A_{g+1}$ and commit it. Every correct replica decides the
same new view $v^+=(g+1,b+1)$ and hence the same next leader $L(v^+)$.}

\item \textbf{First vote.} \RR{Install $A_{g+1}$ as the agreed base,
enter $v^+$, reset the local proposal clock, and resume Figure~\ref{fig:rr-steady}.
$L(v^+)$ immediately broadcasts a fresh child of $A_{g+1}$, justified by
$\decide_g$. Recipients finishing agreement buffer it until their own
entry. The anchor is already agreed and committed: no leader-selected
$\langle\mathrm{new\text{-}view},v^+,\cdot\rangle_L$ message and no anchor
re-certification vote are sent. Old-generation proofs cannot restart this
recovery.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{mdframed}
\captionof{figure}{Failure recovery. The $\Delta$ wait remains, but a common
report vector replaces the new leader's unilateral anchor choice. The
resulting view and committed anchor are common to correct replicas.}
\label{fig:rr-change}
\end{minipage}

\medskip
\textbf{Precise recovery timing.} If the first correct freeze is at $a$,
proof relay gives $a_r\in[a,a+\Delta]$. A node still finishing the
previous generation buffers the new proof until entry; those entry times
differ by at most $\Delta$ after their common decision, so the same freeze
bound holds inductively. Each correct node snapshots at
$u_r=a_r+\Delta$. A correct round-$q$ chain sent at
$u_i+2(q-1)\Delta$ reaches node $r$ by its round-$q$ deadline because
$u_i+2(q-1)\Delta+\Delta\leq u_r+2q\Delta$.
Early chains are buffered. The signed-chain agreement therefore produces
the same vector everywhere by the local deadline
$u_r+2(f+1)\Delta$; all decisions finish by $a+(2f+4)\Delta$.
\RR{The recovery generation is a signing domain: returning to the same
leader term never reuses a previous view's votes.}

\textbf{Equivocation in the two protocols.} Figure~\ref{fig:rr-steady}(2)
compares signed PROPOSE histories across the entire active generation,
including earlier terms. At the first incompatible canonical height the
scheduled leader has signed two different blocks at one view and position.
The proof is sent immediately and Figure~\ref{fig:rr-change}(i) cancels
pending timers. \RR{The original paper additionally checks two
leader-signed NEWVIEW announcements for distinct anchors. RR's agreement
selects its anchor locally at every correct replica and sends no such
leader-selected announcement, so that specific NEWVIEW equivocation message
does not exist here. An unrecognized attempted NEWVIEW is rejected; leader
silence is handled by BLAME.}

\textbf{Guarantee and application boundary.}
The safety bridge is that a correct local $2\Delta$ timer that actually
commits $B_k$ cannot have missed an earlier valid generation freeze or
signed fork: either would reach its owner before expiry. Every correct
member therefore knows $\cert_v(B_k)$ by that time, and no incompatible
current-generation certificate can be assembled. The $\Delta$ wait before
STATUS includes the protecting certificate; the common highest certified
block extends every already committed prefix. Committing that anchor also
prevents repeated faulty successors from indefinitely cancelling all
pending timers while certified height grows. These claims require the
complete-history and common-agreement rules above.

This document formalizes the emph{count-based} standard-synchronous SMR
case. It does not by itself instantiate the regional service in
\texttt{main\_v3.tex}: that source uses faulty stake $<W_i/3$, vote QCs
$>2W_i/3$, BLAME $>W_i/3$, mini-block/application validation,
post-commit CONFIRM receipts and L1 equivocation adjudication. Those
application rules and the weighted proof remain separate obligations.
